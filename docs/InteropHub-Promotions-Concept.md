# InteropHub Promotions

## Purpose

Use selected spaces within InteropHub to introduce community projects, ideas, resources, and opportunities to contribute. These promotions help participants discover relevant work while using the application for meetings and other activities.

Promotions are community communications. They borrow the compact, visual format of advertisements without introducing commercial advertising. A participant can notice one, follow its link, or ignore it and continue with their task.

This document captures the product idea and its first proposed campaign. Tooling, administration, and implementation will be considered later.

## The experience we want

People often arrive in InteropHub with an immediate purpose: recording attendance, reviewing an agenda, or finding a resource. A secondary space can introduce something useful that they might not otherwise encounter. Over time, these small exposures can build familiarity with the community's work and shared practices.

The promotion should feel relevant and welcoming. It should offer a clear idea and an optional path to learn more. Participants do not need a visible label identifying the content as a “promotion”; that term can remain an internal organizing concept.

Examples of future subjects include:

- A new interoperability project seeking participation.
- A demonstration or testing resource available to the community.
- An emerging idea ready for discussion.
- An opportunity to present experience or contribute expertise.
- Shared principles that support community collaboration.

## Placement and behavior

Promotions remain secondary to the work participants came to do. Attendance controls, agendas, meeting links, and resources retain priority.

Two initial placement ideas are:

| Placement | Possible format | Intended experience |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Below attendance submission controls | A wide image with brief introductory text | Optional content in the additional page space, generally below the fold |
| Beside a meeting agenda | A compact vertical promotion featuring one idea | A readable invitation alongside the agenda without displacing it |

Most meeting participants are expected to use a computer. Desktop readability is the initial priority. Exact dimensions should follow the available page layout; a sidebar promotion need not become an excessively tall banner.

Keep the presentation quiet and stable. Avoid animation, automatic carousels, pop-ups, forced scrolling, or layout shifts. One relevant promotion in a placement is preferable to several competing messages. Viewing or clicking a promotion must never become a requirement for completing attendance or accessing meeting content.

Where useful, select promotions appropriate to the meeting or topic space. The selection mechanism remains undecided. Relevance does not require a sophisticated personalization system.

## Campaigns and destinations

A campaign connects several related promotional pieces to a shared subject and destination. For example, a campaign may have one wide overview image and several smaller pieces, each highlighting a single idea.

The promotional piece should be understandable at a glance. Its destination provides the fuller explanation and any appropriate next step. Depending on the subject, that destination might be a topic page, project overview, resource, or dedicated explanatory page.

This is a conceptual relationship rather than a proposed database or component design. Start with a useful campaign and evaluate the experience before deciding what management tools are needed.

## First campaign

### Principles of Immunization Collaboration

The first campaign introduces principles gathered through years of working together in the immunization community. Present them as established shared experience: these are approaches that have helped the community collaborate successfully.

The campaign should feel familiar, confident, and welcoming. It should not suggest that participants need remedial instruction or that the application is introducing a new conduct-enforcement process.

The agreed list is:

1. Everyone is an SME and has something unique to contribute.
2. Assume positive intent, we are all here to help make things better.
3. Dare to make mistakes.
4. Give your ideas away don't own them.
5. Don't let perfect be the enemy of good.
6. Say it once, all perspectives are being captured.

Use these six principles. The expanded seven-principle “Working Well Together” list explored previously was not adopted. Do not substitute that list or its title in this campaign. Shortened display treatments may use line breaks and supporting text while preserving the meaning and recognizable phrasing of the agreed statements.

### Visual direction

Use the coffee-shop chalkboard direction: hand lettering, a dark chalk surface, restrained decorative flourishes, and a friendly handmade character. The statements should dominate the design.

The earlier illustrations featuring children felt juvenile, and the adult workshop illustrations still felt corporate to colleagues. Neither is the intended direction for this campaign.

A chalkboard concept image already exists, but its heading is “Working Well Together.” Any final campaign version needs the agreed heading, **Principles of Immunization Collaboration**. This document records that requirement; it does not imply that the revised asset has already been produced.

### Campaign pieces

| Piece | Content | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full 16:9 visual | Campaign title and all six principles | Introduce the complete set in wide application spaces and meeting slides |
| Smaller vertical pieces | One prominent principle, a small campaign identifier, and a quiet invitation to explore the full set | Make individual statements noticeable and memorable beside an agenda |
| Explanatory webpage | Full visual, the principles as selectable text, brief context, and short explanations | Give participants a durable place to understand and revisit the principles |

Design the smaller pieces for their available width. Do not shrink the full six-principle slide into a sidebar. Each piece should give its featured statement enough room to read comfortably. A possible link label is **Explore all six principles**.

The destination page should explain that the principles emerged from years of community collaboration. It can briefly clarify each statement without becoming a policy manual. The more detailed facilitator guidance serves a separate audience and purpose.

Select the featured principle in advance of a meeting or through an established rotation. Do not change it in response to someone's behavior during a call. A statement such as “Say it once” should remain a shared reminder rather than appearing to target a participant.

## Relationship to facilitation

The campaign creates familiarity and gives facilitators a recognizable reference. It complements spoken introductions and occasional discussion of the principles. Simply displaying them does not establish that every participant has read or understood them.

The explanatory page can preserve the positive intent of the statements. Facilitators remain responsible for applying them fairly, including making sure perspectives really are understood and captured when invoking “Say it once.”

## Decisions for later

Review these questions when considering implementation:

- Which existing pages have appropriate space for promotions?
- How should a campaign's wide and narrow pieces fit those placements?
- Who selects and maintains promotions, and how are outdated items retired?
- Should selection be manual, associated with a meeting or topic space, or rotated on a simple schedule?
- Can existing topic pages and content tools provide the destinations?
- What minimal tooling would make the first campaign easy to maintain?

No particular schema, administration interface, targeting system, analytics feature, or publishing workflow is prescribed here. Those choices should follow a review of the existing application and experience with the first campaign.

## Initial scope and signs of success

Begin with the principles campaign and a small number of suitable placements. Develop the campaign's visual treatment and destination content before expanding into a general promotion-management system.

The initial experience is successful when the content is readable, relevant, and easy to explore, while the underlying attendance and agenda tasks remain straightforward. Participants and facilitators should be able to recognize and refer to the principles over time. Visibility is useful only if the promotions continue to respect the attention and purpose of the community.
